TAPEOUT — FEATURES AND CAPABILITIES
====================================

Tapeout is an iPad app that teaches chip design. You write Verilog on one side
and watch it become a chip on the other: a 3D model, a schematic, a routed
layout, and finally a die on a silicon wafer. It is a Swift Playgrounds app
(SwiftUI, UIKit and SceneKit, iOS 17 or later) and works fully offline.


1. HOME SCREEN
--------------
- Two Liquid Glass tiles: "Learn" (the guided course) and "Editor" (a free
  workspace).
- An Import button that opens the Files picker.
    - A Verilog file (.v, .sv) opens in the editor.
    - A waveform file (.vcd) opens in a full-screen waveform viewer.
- Both workspaces stay alive, so your work survives a trip back to the home
  screen.


2. WORKSPACE LAYOUT
-------------------
- Chip on the left, code on the right, with a divider you drag to resize
  (20% to 80%).
- In portrait or a narrow window the two panes stack vertically.
- Black and dark-gray theme. White is the only highlight; red appears only for
  errors.
- Every button, label and spacing scales with the window size, from Split View
  and iPad mini up to a 13-inch iPad (see section 12).


3. CODE EDITOR
--------------
- Monospaced editor with Verilog syntax highlighting.
- Recompiles as you type. Errors show in a status bar and tint the offending
  line.
- An extra key row above the keyboard for common Verilog symbols.
- Cross-probing: tap a cell or signal anywhere and its source line is
  highlighted.
- An Examples menu in the editor: 8-bit counter, UART sender, and a Tiny
  Tapeout CPU.


4. VERILOG THE APP UNDERSTANDS
------------------------------
- Several modules in one file, with module instances (the design is flattened).
- Buses up to 64 bits, wire and reg, memories (reg [7:0] ram [0:15]),
  parameters and localparams.
- assign, including assignments to single bits and part-selects.
- always @(posedge clk) with non-blocking assignments (registers and memory
  writes).
- always @(*) combinational blocks, with a clear error if a latch would be
  inferred.
- if / else and case statements.
- Operators: + - * / %, comparisons, shifts, bitwise and logical operators,
  reduction operators, the ?: conditional, bit and part selects,
  concatenation and replication.
- Gate primitives (and, or, xor, nand, nor, xnor, not, buf).
- Sized literals in binary, hex, decimal and octal. Compiler directives such as
  `default_nettype are skipped.

Limits: one clock; registers start at 0; x and z are treated as 0; values are
unsigned; an asynchronous reset is treated as synchronous. Not supported: for
loops, generate, functions and tasks, inout ports, initial blocks, signed
arithmetic, and parameter overrides on instances.


5. SYNTHESIS AND SIMULATION
---------------------------
- Synthesises the code into a word-level netlist: adders, comparators, muxes,
  shifters, registers and RAM as single blocks, with constant folding and
  shared sub-expressions.
- Simulates the real netlist, cycle by cycle. Nothing is faked: the lamps,
  waveforms and register values all come from evaluating your design.
- Clocked designs: Step one clock edge, Run continuously, or Clear back to
  zero.
- Combinational designs: toggle inputs and the outputs settle immediately.


6. FOUR VIEWS OF THE CHIP
-------------------------
3D
- A tasteful 3D chip: package, die, bond pads, cells and metal wires on four
  stacked layers. Wires light up when they carry a 1. Buses are drawn taller.
- Orbit, pinch to zoom, tap an input pad to toggle it, tap a cell to select it.

Schematic
- The elaborated design drawn with standard gate symbols, arithmetic blocks,
  registers and memory, laid out by logic depth.
- Pan and pinch. Tap a signal to select it.
- Cone tracing: show everything that feeds a signal (fan-in), everything it
  drives (fan-out), or both. Tracing stops at registers.

Layout
- The placed and routed design from above: cells, pads, three routing layers
  (M1 to M3), an M4 layer for register feedback, and vias.
- Layer toggles for cells, each metal layer and vias.
- A ruler: tap two points to measure a distance.
- Density setting (Compact, Normal, Roomy) that re-places and re-routes.
- Overlays: routing congestion heat map, timing (critical path), and signal
  activity.
- Design-rule markers drawn on the layout, numbered to match the Checks list.

Wafer
- Your chip as it would leave a fab, at four zoom levels:
    1. The wafer: a silicon disc with a flat (100 and 150 mm) or a notch
       (200 and 300 mm), a 3 mm edge-exclusion ring, and your die stepped
       across it with saw lanes between copies. Partial dies at the rim are
       greyed out.
    2. A yield map: each die passes or fails from a Poisson model of die area
       and the defect density you pick. Failed dies are marked in red.
    3. One die: seal ring, labelled bond pads (one per port bit plus VDD and
       VSS), power and ground rings, power straps, and a separate wire from
       every pad to its pin on the core.
    4. The standard cells, in the style of a Tiny Tapeout layout render: rows
       of cells with diffusion, polysilicon gates and local interconnect,
       shared power rails, decap and fill cells, well taps, a clock tree, and
       signal routing on three metal layers, with labelled pins along the top
       edge.
- The design is broken into single-bit standard cells sized like the SkyWater
  130 nm library (0.46 um sites, 2.72 um rows), placed in rows, and fitted to
  the smallest Tiny Tapeout tile it fits (1x1 up to 8x2) at no more than 60%
  full.
- Buttons jump straight to Wafer, Die or Cells. Menus set the wafer size (100,
  150, 200, 300 mm) and defect density.
- A summary shows core size and tile, cell and flip-flop counts, how full the
  tile is, total wire length, die size, dies per wafer, estimated yield and
  good dies.

The wafer view is an estimate, not a foundry result. See section 13.


7. THE DOCK (BELOW THE CHIP)
----------------------------
Bench
- Clock controls and a cycle counter.
- Switches for 1-bit inputs and per-bit toggles for buses.
- Output lamps and bus values.
- Live register values and memory contents.
- A full truth table for small single-bit designs, with the current row
  marked. In lessons, wrong rows are flagged.

Waves
- A waveform for every input, output and register.
- Clocked designs record one sample per clock step; combinational designs show
  a delay-accurate sweep of every input combination.
- Radix per view: binary, hex, unsigned decimal, signed decimal, ASCII and
  floating point.
- Protocol decoding: UART, SPI and I2C. Pick which signal plays each role
  (the app guesses from the names) and decoded bytes appear as labelled frames.
- Analog rows for real-valued signals from imported files.
- A time cursor you drag; tapping a row selects that signal in every view.

Timing
- Static timing analysis using a delay per cell type.
- The critical path listed cell by cell, with the arrival time at each step.
- A timing budget you set, and the slack for every output against it.

Checks
- Design-rule check: minimum spacing and shorts between wires of different
  nets. Tap a violation to jump to it on the layout.
- Lint: inputs that are never used, outputs stuck at a constant, and signals
  with a high fan-out.


8. IMPORTED WAVEFORMS
---------------------
- Reads VCD files, including bus and real (analog) signals.
- The same viewer as the Waves tab: radix switching, the time cursor, and
  UART, SPI and I2C decoding.
- Large files are resampled (up to 4,000 samples and 300 signals).


9. THE COURSE (LEARN MODE)
--------------------------
Thirteen lessons, each with a short concept, a goal, starter code, a hint and
a worked solution. Each one is checked against your actual design, not by
matching text.

  1 to 7   Gates and combinational logic: your first wire, the inverter, AND,
           XOR, a half adder, a multiplexer and a full adder.
  8        Reading a waveform.
  9        Tracing a cone of influence in the schematic.
  10       Finding the critical path and meeting a timing budget.
  11       Metal layers and layer toggles in the layout.
  12       Fixing a design-rule violation by changing the density.
  13       Free build.

A verdict badge shows whether the lesson is passed, what is still wrong, or
what to do next. Lessons can be reset.


10. CROSS-PROBING
-----------------
Selecting a signal in any one place highlights it everywhere: the 3D chip,
the schematic, the layout, the waveform row and the source line.


11. BUILT-IN EXAMPLES
---------------------
- 8-bit counter.
- UART sender (decodes to the letter 'A' in the Waves tab).
- Tiny CPU: a Tiny Tapeout project with a program counter, two registers, a
  16-byte RAM and a small instruction set. It becomes 64 word-level blocks, or
  765 standard cells with 155 flip-flops, fits a 1x1 Tiny Tapeout tile at 59%
  full, and gives a 1.32 x 1.32 mm die.


12. SCREEN SIZES
----------------
- The app measures its window and scales text, buttons, tap targets and
  spacing together: 1.0 on an 11-inch iPad, down to 0.8 in Split View or on an
  iPad mini, up to 1.3 on a 13-inch iPad.
- Rows of buttons scroll sideways when they do not fit.
- The dock takes a share of the height, within a minimum and maximum.
- The code editor's text stays at one size so code is always readable.


13. WHAT IT DOES NOT DO
-----------------------
- It does not produce files a foundry can manufacture from (no GDSII export),
  and it is not a signoff tool.
- Standard-cell counts, placement and routing in the wafer view are estimates.
  A real flow would optimise the logic and route every net to exact rules, and
  the detailed routing here is not design-rule checked.
- Die size assumes 60 um bond pads on a 90 um pitch. Yield uses a simple
  Poisson model.
- No LVS, IR-drop, thermal or clock-skew analysis, and no state-machine
  diagrams.
- Waveform import is VCD only (no FST, FSDB or WLF). Protocol decoding covers
  UART, SPI and I2C (no AXI or Wishbone).
- The app has been compiled for iPad and its views rendered on a Mac, but it
  has not yet been run on an iPad or in the simulator, so touch gestures, the
  file picker and the Liquid Glass effect are untested.
